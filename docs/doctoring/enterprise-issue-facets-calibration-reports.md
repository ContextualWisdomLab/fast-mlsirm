# Doctoring record: enterprise issue facets calibration reports

## Purpose

This record documents the bounded issue #544 adapter that converts governed
enterprise issue executions into the existing shared criterion-specific
many-facet calibration reports. The adapter advances the larger issue #404
vertical and introduces no statistical equation or report schema.

## Architecture decision

The enterprise module performs only five operations:

1. validate and assemble exact executions through
   `build_enterprise_issue_facets_calibration_bundle()`;
2. normalize source-free caller metadata and review-trigger identifiers;
3. derive and validate every criterion report identifier before any estimator
   delegation;
4. bind each criterion report to the exact bundle, design, and criterion
   fingerprint or identifier; and
5. delegate every design to `fit_scoring_facets_calibration_report()`.

All likelihood, quadrature, gradient, Hessian, parameter-update, optimization,
and fit replay remain in the existing Rust-backed many-facet implementation. The
returned `ScoringFacetsCalibrationReport` is the exact compatibility alias of the
established report class. No enterprise-specific report, fit, result, rating, or
design object is created.

## Equation-to-source traceability

This adapter adds no equation. The delegated many-facet model and estimator
traceability remain authoritative in the automated essay facets calibration
documentation.

Bock and Aitkin (1981) provide the marginal maximum-likelihood and EM foundation
used by the delegated estimator family. Linacre (1989) and Eckes (2015) provide
many-facet Rasch measurement foundations and rater-mediated interpretation
boundaries. These sources do not, by themselves, validate an enterprise use case,
a construct, a rater population, or a deployment policy.

## Testing and interpretation boundary

The *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* distinguish technical
score production from evidence supporting an intended interpretation and use.
Accordingly, a report generated by this adapter establishes only package-level
provenance and fit replay. It does not establish construct validity, reliability,
fairness, rater interchangeability, predictive validity, model adequacy, or
high-stakes readiness.

A realistic connected fixture crosses the actual Rust-backed fitting path, while
deterministic fixture fits verify exact orchestration and metadata behavior. The
suite also proves execution-order invariance, one-time trigger normalization,
batch rejection of invalid derived report identities before fitting,
reserved-metadata rejection, and absence of raw source and issue text from report
serialization.

## AI governance boundary

Enterprise observations may be produced by automated or human judges, but this
adapter calls no LLM and stores no private reasoning. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 describes
organizational AI management-system requirements, including risk management and
continual improvement. NIST AI 600-1 provides a generative-AI risk profile for
trustworthy design, use, and evaluation. The adapter contributes technical
traceability and review controls; it does not claim conformity with either
publication.

Nonconvergence and disconnectedness remain mandatory shared review triggers.
Additional organizational review triggers are normalized once and cannot suppress
those structural warnings.

## Privacy and rollback

Raw source text, issue statements, prompts, provider responses, customer tokens,
and credentials are not accepted as package-managed report provenance. Existing
metadata validation rejects sensitive source and response fields. The adapter
adds only content fingerprints, design fingerprints, criterion identifiers, and
caller-owned source-free metadata.

Rollback removes the additive enterprise reporting module, exports,
documentation, and tests. Persisted shared report artifacts require no migration
because their class, handle, fingerprint, serialization, and wire identity are
unchanged.

## Verification contract

Merge requires one unchanged head to pass:

- actual Rust-backed connected fitting;
- exact enterprise bundle/design/criterion report provenance;
- execution-order invariance;
- tuning, trigger, and metadata delegation;
- atomic derived-report-identifier validation before estimator delegation;
- reserved metadata and invalid identifier failures;
- source and issue text privacy assertions;
- 100% new production statement and branch coverage;
- complete public docstrings;
- full Python, Rust/PyO3, package, GPU-no-skip, fuzz, Security Scan, and SAST
  workflows;
- authoritative changelog parity;
- current-head review, independent approval, zero actionable unresolved threads,
  and branch protection.
